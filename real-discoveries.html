Real Ocean Discoveries

Meet the tools and discoveries helping scientists understand places humans cannot easily visit.

Hydrothermal vents

Discovered in the late 1970s, these seafloor features release hot, mineral-rich fluids. Entire ecosystems can thrive around them without relying directly on sunlight.

Deep-sea ecosystems

New species

Scientists continue to describe new marine species from samples and footage collected by research vessels, submersibles and remotely operated vehicles.

Biodiversity

Mapping the seafloor

Sonar sends sound pulses and measures their echoes to help map underwater landscapes, including ridges, canyons and trenches.

Ocean mapping

Life in the darkest places

Research has found animals living in deep-sea trenches. Their adaptations help scientists learn how life copes with extreme pressure and limited food.

Extreme environments

How researchers explore

  • Sonar maps large areas of seafloor.
  • ROVs carry cameras and instruments.
  • Submersibles take people to selected deep sites.
  • Water and sediment samples reveal chemistry and organisms.

Still unexplored

Large parts of the ocean remain poorly observed in detail. Better maps and more expeditions can lead to new questions as well as answers.
